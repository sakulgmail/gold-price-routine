## Key Reasons

- **NFP July 2 is the binary directional trigger:** June's Nonfarm Payrolls (Thursday July 2 — moved from Friday due to US Independence Day) will determine gold's near-term path. Goldman Sachs forecasts 60K jobs; EY-Parthenon forecasts 50K; market consensus is ~80K. A weak print collapses the September rate-hike premium (~62% probability), weakens the DXY, and targets $4,200–$4,360 for gold. A strong print (>100K) reinforces Fed hawkishness and sends gold back toward $3,900–$4,000 support.
- **Bearish macro consensus; oversold technical condition:** 44% of Wall Street and 46% of Main Street are bearish into the week, driven by PCE at 4.1% YoY and jobless claims at 215K. However, gold dropped roughly 3% for the week (from $4,142 open to a $3,959 mid-week low) before recovering — multiple analysts flagged the selloff as "overdone" and the oversold condition as creating upside snap-back risk.
- **Technical: stuck between $3,952 support and $4,114 resistance:** LiteFinance's weekly range for June 29–July 5 is $3,952–$4,114. Current price ~$4,088 sits just below near-term resistance at $4,100–$4,130. A sustained close above $4,130 opens $4,157 then $4,221 (last week's high). A break below $3,952 targets CPM Group's $3,800 bearish objective.
- **Geopolitical wildcard (Strait of Hormuz):** Friday's +$103.60 XAU/USD recovery was partly driven by renewed US-Iran tensions in the Strait of Hormuz. If weekend diplomatic developments ease the confrontation, the safe-haven geopolitical premium may partially unwind on Monday's open, creating early-week downside risk.
- **Structural demand floor remains intact:** World Gold Council data: 89% of reserve managers expect global central bank gold holdings to increase within 12 months. Bank of America maintains its $6,000 long-term target; J.P. Morgan forecasts $5,000–$6,300 by end of 2026 and into 2027. Structural buyers continue to absorb pressure near the $4,000 level, capping the downside.
