## Key Reasons

- **Fourth consecutive weekly loss but strong Friday reversal (primary framing):** XAU/USD dropped roughly 3% for the week, hitting a 7-month low near $3,969 mid-week before staging a powerful Friday recovery to $4,088.60 (+1.55%). The recovery was data-driven (soft PCE on June 26) and geopolitically amplified (Trump/Iran tensions). The weekly close above $4,000 is technically meaningful but does not signal a trend reversal.
- **NFP on July 2 is the binary catalyst for next week:** If June payrolls come in weak (Goldman Sachs forecasts 60,000; EY-Parthenon 50,000), the September rate-hike probability will drop sharply, the DXY should weaken, and gold bulls will target $4,130–$4,180 then $4,360. A strong print (>100,000) would revive the hawkish narrative and push gold back toward $3,900–$4,000 support.
- **Technical picture: range-bound with downside risk:** Current price ~$4,088 sits just below near-term resistance at $4,100–$4,130. The 200-day SMA at $4,474 is ~9% above. Support at $4,000 (psychological) and $3,816 (October 2025 low zone). InterGold warns a break below $4,000 could accelerate toward $3,500.
- **Structural demand floor remains intact:** YLG data: 45% of global central banks plan to increase gold holdings; gold is trading ~30% below its January 2026 all-time high of $5,589; Goldman Sachs year-end target $4,900; J.P. Morgan $5,000+ by Q4 2026 / Q1 2027.
- **DXY headwind persists:** The Dollar Index remains near its 2026 high, making gold more expensive for foreign buyers and providing the chief structural headwind. Any softening of the DXY — whether from weak NFP, diplomatic de-escalation, or Fed pivot signals — is the clearest near-term catalyst for gold upside.
