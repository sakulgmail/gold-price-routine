## Today's Prediction
**Direction: ▼ DOWN** | Confidence: Medium

Global spot XAU/USD has fallen roughly -1.3% overnight, from about $4,345 at yesterday's Thai-close reference to about $4,288 now, and remains pinned below the psychologically important $4,300 level — the single highest-weighted signal, and it points clearly DOWN. The decline was driven by a stronger dollar and surging Treasury yields after Wednesday's hot flash PMI print reinforced expectations that the Fed has less room to cut, a theme that has now pressured gold for two straight sessions. USD/THB has weakened only marginally (~33.23 → ~33.25), far too small a baht-side tailwind to offset the global move. FXStreet — now the highest-accuracy tracked source (3-for-3 over its last three gradable sessions) — flagged exactly this outcome Wednesday, first framing a "bull-bear tug-of-war" and then, in a same-day follow-up, explicitly noting "bears gaining traction" as price drifted toward $4,300; that bearish drift has now extended overnight. YLG's Wednesday note flagged a break of the $4,322 support with a bounce, conditional on holding a higher low above $4,291 to argue the pullback was over — spot has now traded through that $4,291 pivot to $4,288, invalidating the bullish-bounce condition and reinforcing further downside risk. The Gold Research Center's bullish retail survey (58% up) has been wrong two days running and carries almost no weight. The Trump–Xi summit's formal talks and state dinner land in Washington during US daytime hours, i.e. after today's Thai session closes, so there is no direct same-day scheduled-event risk during Thai trading — but markets are visibly reluctant to take fresh positions ahead of the outcome, and a headline surprise (positive or negative) could hit late in the Asian evening or spill into tomorrow. That event-risk overhang caps confidence at Medium rather than High.
