## Key Reasons

- **Technical bounce, not trend reversal:** Gold fell below all four major moving averages last week (unusual, per LHC analyst Pattarin Wachirakhaphan). Monday's +700 baht is an oversold relief rally; historically such setups reverse within 1–2 months, but the near-term path remains under pressure.
- **Iran nuclear talks collapsed:** Iran walked out of Switzerland negotiations after US threats, reigniting geopolitical risk and safe-haven demand. However, higher Hormuz Strait risk → higher oil → higher inflation → Fed hawkishness sustained — a double-edged sword for gold.
- **SPDR gold ETF institutional bid:** The SPDR fund added +7 tonnes on Friday June 19, signalling institutional support and providing a near-term floor.
- **Hawkish Fed ceiling:** Fed Chair Kevin Warsh left rates at 3.50–3.75% but signalled a 25 bps rate hike before year-end remains possible. Elevated USD and Treasury yields are structural headwinds for non-yielding gold.
- **Resistance at $4,220–$4,250 held intraday:** Gold tested this zone during Monday's session but failed to close above it. Breaking above would signal a trend reversal; remaining below keeps the bearish technical structure intact.
- **Critical data week:** Flash PMIs (June 23), Q1 GDP / Core PCE (June 25), UMich Inflation (June 26). Strong readings reinforce the Fed rate-hike scenario and add downside pressure. Probability of unchanged Fed rates at next meeting: 99.4%, but guidance beyond is uncertain.
- **Institutional downgrade:** Goldman Sachs cut its 2026 year-end gold target to $4,900/oz from $5,300/oz, citing lower ETF inflows and delayed Fed rate cuts.
- **Long-term bullish case intact:** J.P. Morgan targets $6,000; LiteFinance weekly range forecast $3,894–$4,761; global gold demand in Q1 2026 was the highest January–March on record (1,231 tonnes). The long-term structure is bullish, but medium-term macro headwinds dominate.
