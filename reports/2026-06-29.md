## Key Reasons

- **NFP Thursday July 2 is the binary catalyst for the week:** June payrolls release Thursday July 2 (moved from the usual Friday due to the US July 4 Independence Day observed on Friday July 3). Consensus ~80,000; Goldman Sachs 60,000; EY-Parthenon 50,000. A weak print collapses the September rate-hike premium and could send gold to $4,130–$4,180; a strong print (>100,000) risks a retest of $3,950–$4,000 support and CPM Group's $3,800 target comes into view.
- **Technical "death cross" and four-week losing streak:** Gold's 50-day SMA has crossed below the 200-day SMA — a classically bearish technical signal. XAU/USD is now ~28% below its January 2026 all-time high of $5,589, and four consecutive weekly losses have accumulated a total decline of roughly 13% from the peak.
- **US-Iran peace talks reduce safe-haven premium:** US-Iran negotiations resumed in Doha with a temporary attack suspension. Progress in talks would erode the geopolitical risk premium that has partly supported gold. Strait of Hormuz shipping concerns persist (shipping firms flagged fresh safety anxiety), but the diplomatic momentum is net-negative for safe-haven demand near term.
- **SPDR Gold Trust sold 15.41 tonnes:** Institutional ETF outflows represent short-term de-risking by Western investors and add direct selling pressure. Combined with DXY stability and hawkish Fed commentary, this creates multiple headwinds simultaneously.
- **Structural demand floor intact but insufficient near-term:** Central bank buying (PBOC at 8 t/month as of April), Goldman Sachs $4,900 year-end target, and J.P. Morgan $5,000–$6,000 by Q4 2026 provide a longer-term floor. YLG's buy-on-dip recommendation at $3,960 reflects this view. However, structural demand alone cannot overcome the near-term hawkish Fed + strong DXY combination in the week ahead.
