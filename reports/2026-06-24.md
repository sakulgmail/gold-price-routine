## Key Reasons

- **$4,000 psychological support breached:** Gold trading below $4,000 for the first time since November 2025 — a major technical deterioration likely to trigger further stop-loss selling and reduce institutional buy interest.
- **"Debasement trade" narrative unwinds (primary driver):** Markets are reassessing the thesis that persistent fiscal deficits guarantee prolonged monetary accommodation. With the Fed pivoting hawkish, the gold-as-inflation-hedge trade is losing its narrative anchor (CoinDesk, June 24).
- **Hawkish Fed — two hikes now priced by March 2027:** Nine Fed officials signalled at least one rate increase this year at the June meeting. Markets now price 2×25 bps hikes (fed funds rate rising to 4.00%–4.25%), raising the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold.
- **US-Iran deal eases geopolitical premium:** 60-day sanctions relief, repatriation of $12 billion, and progress on Strait of Hormuz re-opening reduces the geopolitical "fear premium" embedded in gold prices.
- **Strong Flash PMI (52.2) dampens safe-haven demand:** The S&P Global Flash US Composite PMI rose to 52.2 (Manufacturing 55.7, a 4-year high), reducing recession fears and supporting risk-on sentiment at gold's expense.
- **Institutional downgrades:** Goldman Sachs cut year-end 2026 target to $4,900 (from $5,400); Deutsche Bank cut Q3 to $4,300; Morgan Stanley maintains $5,200 contingent on ETF inflows.
- **PCE data is the next catalyst (Jun 25–26):** If Core PCE prints hot (≥3.6%), the hawkish narrative intensifies and gold risks testing the next support zone at $3,878–$3,933. A soft reading (≤3.4%) could trigger a relief bounce.
