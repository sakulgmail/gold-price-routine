> **Note on timing:** This report was compiled early in the Bangkok morning, before the GTA's first intraday price announcement (~09:00–09:15 Bangkok time). The call below is therefore made from the overnight global setup and leading signals, in the same way the July 6 and July 7 morning-first calls were — not from same-day Thai price action.

## Today's Prediction
**Direction: ▼ DOWN** | Confidence: High

Overnight, Iran's Revolutionary Guard fired missiles at a Qatari LNG carrier (the Nakilat-owned *Al Rekayyat*) in the Strait of Hormuz, and the U.S. retaliated with a fresh wave of strikes on Iranian port cities (Bandar Abbas, Sirik, Qeshm Island) while revoking its temporary waiver on Iranian oil sales (wind-down required by July 17). Global spot XAU/USD extended Tuesday's slide, falling from Tuesday's ~$4,165 close to an intraday low near **$4,116** (around **$4,095–$4,130** as of this writing, roughly **−1% to −1.6%**) as the attack pushed oil (WTI ~$69.6, Brent ~$73.1, both +1.5–1.6%) and Treasury yields (10-year ~4.55%) higher and revived September rate-hike odds to ~53% (from ~50% right after last week's weak payrolls, though still below the pre-payrolls ~66%). This is the same pattern that has now correctly called gold DOWN for two straight days: in this market, a Hormuz escalation reads as an energy-inflation shock that keeps the Fed hawkish, not a safe-haven bid for gold. USD/THB firmed only mildly (~33.25 → ~33.33), a small offsetting tailwind for baht-gold that is nowhere near large enough to offset a >1% global drop. The Fed's June-meeting minutes are due today at 2 p.m. ET (~1 a.m. Bangkok, July 9) — after Thailand's session closes — so it adds volatility risk for tomorrow rather than capping today's call. With the overnight move this large and unambiguous, and the Hormuz-driven playbook validated on both July 6 and July 7, confidence is High that today's Thai session opens and closes lower.
