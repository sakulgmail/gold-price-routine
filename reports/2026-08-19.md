## Today's Prediction
**Direction: ▼ DOWN** | Confidence: Medium

Global spot XAU/USD has fallen sharply since yesterday's Thai close, sliding from roughly $4,390–4,400 (the level being tested around Thailand's ~17:30 Bangkok close Tuesday) to about $4,339–4,365 now (Kitco, Mitrade) — a genuine ~1.0–1.3% overnight decline, and gold has broken below the 100-day moving average ($4,384) and the psychologically important $4,400 level. The driver is a surge in US Treasury yields (10-year ~4.71–4.72%, 30-year at its highest since 2007 at 5.33%) ahead of today's FOMC minutes, which has strengthened the dollar and made non-yielding gold less attractive, even as September rate-hike odds have fallen. USD/THB has firmed only modestly to ~33.10–33.12 (from ~33.03–33.04), a partial but small offset — nowhere near enough to counter the global slide. Notably, Thailand's own close was still up +50 baht yesterday even as global spot was already softening into the US afternoon, meaning the Thai price has not yet caught down to the full extent of the overnight global decline — a lagged catch-up move lower looks likely at today's open. Source views reinforce this: InterGold is flagging bond-yield pressure and telling investors to wait for a pullback rather than chase, with support at $4,360/68,500 and secondary support at $4,300–4,320; FXStreet's prior bullish-above-$4,400 framework has now been technically broken, with new support at $4,300 and further downside risk to $4,202; and the Gold Research Center's weekly survey already leans bearish (46% of experts down vs. 31% up). The FOMC minutes themselves land after Thai market hours, which caps confidence at Medium rather than High given the elevated volatility risk into tonight's release.

- Global spot XAU/USD has fallen sharply since yesterday's Thai close (~$4,390–4,400 → ~$4,339–4,365 now, roughly −1.0% to −1.3%), breaking below both the $4,400 level and the 100-day moving average ($4,384) — the single strongest and clearest signal today, and it points down.
